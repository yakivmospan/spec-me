# Second look

Before acting on a go-ahead — "apply", "let's go", "do it" — with no plan agreed, in the
conversation or in an approved plan file, run the `one-head-good-two-better` skill. Not for the
go-ahead of an agreed plan.

Also run it — even mid-task — before adding real scope (a new toggle, mechanism, or design layer) to
guard against a failure mode nobody has actually reported; a hypothetical isn't a reason on its own.

Scope you add yourself counts, and is the easier one to miss: rewriting a file when you were asked to
change a line, or restructuring something on the way past. The test is whether the user would
recognise the work as what they asked for.
